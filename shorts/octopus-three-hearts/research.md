# Research: why octopuses have three hearts

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Two hearts pump blood past the gills (CO2 out, oxygen in); the third sends oxygen-rich blood to organs and muscles | [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/ten-wild-facts-about-octopuses-they-have-three-hearts-big-brains-and-blue-blood-7625828/) |
| Blood is blue because the oxygen carrier, hemocyanin, uses copper instead of iron | [Natural History Museum, London](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/octopuses-keep-surprising-us-here-are-eight-examples-how.html); Smithsonian (above) |
| Hemocyanin carries oxygen more efficiently in cold, low-oxygen conditions | NHM (above); Smithsonian (above) |
| The systemic (body) heart stops while the octopus swims by jet propulsion | Wells, Duthie, Houlihan, Smith & Wells (1987), "Blood flow and pressure changes in exercising octopuses", J. Exp. Biol. 131:175 ([abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1987JExpB.131..175W/abstract)) |
| Swimming is exhausting, so octopuses usually crawl | [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/lifestyle/pets-animals/why-an-octopus-doesnt-die-even-after-its-heart-stops-beating-10418624/); [OctoNation](https://octonation.com/how-many-hearts-does-an-octopus-have/) (secondary) |
