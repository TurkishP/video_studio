# Research: what is love in the Christian sense?

Thesis: Christian love (agapē) is not a feeling you fall into; it is choosing someone's good whatever they give back.

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| The Greek Old Testament uses *eros* only twice; the New Testament never uses it. Of *eros*, *philia* and *agape*, NT writers prefer *agape*, a word "rather infrequent in Greek usage" | Benedict XVI, [*Deus Caritas Est*](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_ben-xvi_enc_20051225_deus-caritas-est.html) §3 (2005) |
| Agape is contrasted in scripture with *eros* (erotic love) and *philia* (brotherly love); it is God's love for humans and its unselfish extension to others | [Britannica, "Agape"](https://www.britannica.com/topic/agape) |
| In the NT, agape is "a way of treating people", not mainly a feeling: seeking others' well-being regardless of their response (1 Cor 13; Luke 6:27-36) | [BibleProject, "Love (Agape)"](https://bibleproject.com/videos/agape-love/) |
| "Love is patient and is kind; love doesn't envy." | 1 Corinthians 13:4, World English Bible (public domain), via bible-api.com |
| "While we were yet sinners, Christ died for us." | Romans 5:8, World English Bible |
| The father runs to his returning son, embraces and kisses him; the son starts his confession and the father cuts in before he finishes it | Luke 15:20-22 |
| The Good Samaritan helps a stranger who cannot repay him | Luke 10:25-37 |
| *Philia* = friendship, *storgē* = family affection, *eros* = desire (common usage; the four-fold scheme was popularised by C.S. Lewis, *The Four Loves*, 1960 — ideas only, not quoted) | Britannica (above); general reference |

Left out: claims that *storgē* never appears in the NT (it appears only in compounds, Rom 1:31, 12:10); too fine-grained for 50 s.
